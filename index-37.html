Pillar One · Mindset

Closing is not pressure.
Closing is leadership.

At the door, the customer doesn’t know what the next step is. They don’t know how solar works. They don’t know if this is worth their time. If you sound unsure — they default to “I’m good, thanks.”

For an appointment setter, closing is simply moving the conversation to the next step. That next step is getting the homeowner to agree to sit down with the closer and see the numbers.

The customer is listening. The conversation is going well. There are clear signs of interest. And then — the setter keeps talking instead of closing. Because closing feels uncomfortable. It feels like pressure.

That is the wrong definition of closing.

01
You are not closing
A solar deal.
02
You are closing
A time slot. A seat at the table.
03
The win looks like
A confirmed appointment with all decision-makers present.
Pillar Two · Script & Structure

The six-step door approach.

Steps one through five are not just an introduction — they are what earns you the right to close. Every step builds permission.

1

Intro

Build rapport. You are not a stranger — you are from the neighborhood. Warmth here creates openness later.

2

Problem

This is where you find the pain. Ask about the bill. React to it. If they feel no pain, they feel no urgency. No urgency, no appointment.

3

Solution

Brief. You are not explaining solar — you are introducing the idea that there is a better option. Point to roofs. Create curiosity.

4

Building Value

The new program removes every objection about cost. No out-of-pocket. No loan. Replaces the bill. This is the “what if this were free” moment.

5

Pullback — The Pre-Close Critical

Only 1 in 4 homes qualify. This creates scarcity and urgency. Deliver this confidently. It is what makes step 6 feel logical.

6

Close + Scheduling The Close

Morning or afternoon. Then 2pm or 4pm. You are not asking if they want an appointment — you are assuming they do.

Script · Step 5 Pullback

Step back. Hands up.

“My job is simple. I am not here to have y’all decide whether you want to go solar or not. I just determine if you and your property would qualify for this brand new program. The hard part is — with this program — typically only 1 out of 4 homes are eligible.”

Timing

When to ask for the business.

Asking at the wrong time will kill an appointment even when the customer is interested. The signals are there — most setters just aren’t trained to see them.

The danger zones. Asking before establishing bill pain — no pain, no motivation. Asking before the pullback — the appointment feels low-value and easy to skip. Over-explaining solar — questions create doubt, doubt kills momentum. Waiting for a “perfect moment” that never comes. Re-opening the conversation after the close — once you’ve asked, be quiet.

  • ✓They answered your bill question and reacted to the number.
  • ✓They didn’t walk away or shut the conversation down during your pitch.
  • ✓They’re still making eye contact and facing you.
  • ✓You have delivered the pullback — the 1-in-4 line.
  • ✓There is a natural pause in the conversation.
“

You don’t need them to say “I’m interested” to go for the close. You need them to still be standing there.

Reading the Customer

Verbal cues at the door.

Ready · Green Light
  • “How does that work?”
  • “Wait, there’s no out-of-pocket cost?”
  • “Yeah, our bill has been crazy.”
  • “My neighbor got solar — I’ve been curious.”
  • “So what’s the catch?” (curiosity, not resistance)
  • “Who would come out?”
  • “Do you need to see my bill?”
  • “Would we qualify?”
Not Ready · Red Flag
  • “We’re renting.”
  • “I need to talk to my spouse.”
  • “We already have solar.”
  • “I’m really busy right now.”
  • “We’re not interested in solar.”
  • “Can you leave something and I’ll look at it?”
  • “We don’t make decisions like that.”
Body Language

Nonverbal cues.

Ready · Open
  • Leaning forward slightly.
  • Nodding while you speak.
  • Sustained eye contact.
  • Body turned toward you.
  • Calling spouse to the door.
  • Looking at neighbors’ roofs when you point.
  • Phone or keys put away.
  • Stepping slightly off the porch.
Not Ready · Closed
  • Arms tightly crossed.
  • Body turned sideways toward door.
  • Hand on the door handle.
  • Looking inside the house repeatedly.
  • Not making eye contact.
  • Checking phone while you talk.
  • Short clipped answers, no follow-up.
  • Stepping back as you continue.
Pillar Three · The Close

The assumptive close.

Speak and act as if the appointment is already happening. You are not asking if they want to meet — you are asking when.

Weak · Asking If
  • “Would you be interested in a consultation?”
  • “Do you want me to set something up?”
  • “Would it be okay if someone came out?”
  • “Are you open to hearing more?”
  • “I could have someone reach out to you…”
Strong · Assumptive
  • “Are you guys more of a morning or afternoon type?”
  • “I have a couple openings — does 2 or 4 work better?”
  • “My energy specialist will be in your area tomorrow.”
  • “I just need a couple quick pieces of info.”
  • “Do you have a corner of a table I can write on?”
Script · Full Assumptive Close

After the Pullback — Step 5

“So my energy specialist will be out here tomorrow going over these reports with some of your neighbors in the morning and later in the afternoon. They basically just sit down and go over: what it is, how it works, and how much you’d be saving if you get selected.”

Assumptive question

“With that said — are you guys more of a morning or afternoon type of people?”

They answer — pull out phone, act busy

“Sounds good. I’ve got a couple of openings left. Does 2pm or 4pm work better for you?”

They pick — confirm, roll forward

“Perfect. Since this is a state program there are a couple of quick qualifying questions…”

Okay, I just need a couple pieces of information and I’ll be out of your hair. Do you have a corner of a table I can write on?

The Rescue Technique

The “No” close.
— Chris Voss method

People are uncomfortable saying YES to a stranger. Yes feels like commitment. Yes feels like they owe something. But No feels safe. No feels like control.

So you ask questions designed to get a No — that actually move them forward. The “No” close is your rescue technique. Use it after the assumptive close has met resistance. Do not lead with it — it works because it feels different from the assumptive.

Script · Deploy the No Close

They say: “I don’t know, I need to think about it.”

Calm, non-reactive, empathetic tone

“Hey, I completely understand. Would it be completely unreasonable just to hear what the numbers look like? No pressure, no obligation. If it does not make sense, you just say no and that’s it. Would that be completely out of the question?”

— or —

“Is it that you are against seeing what you might be saving?”

— or —

“Would it be fair to at least see if your home qualifies before deciding?”

01You deliver the assumptive close“Morning or afternoon? Does 2 or 4 work better?”
02Customer shows resistance“I don’t know · I need to think · maybe later”
03Deploy the No close“Would it be completely unreasonable…”
04aThey re-engage (common)Continue to qualifying questions, go inside.
04bThey hold firmDo not push. Move to graceful exit.
05Graceful exit“Not every home is the right fit. I appreciate your time.”
Pillar Four · Objections & Looping

An objection is a speed bump.
Not a stop sign.

Most reps don’t lose appointments because the customer had a brilliant objection. They lose because they get nervous, explain too much, give away control, or forget to close again after answering.

01
Step One

Acknowledge

Show them you heard the concern. Stay calm and human.

“Totally fair.”
02
Step Two

Validate

Make the concern feel normal instead of arguing with it.

“Most people feel the same way at first.”
03
Step Three

Reframe

Move from buying solar to seeing if the numbers make sense.

“You are not deciding today.”
04
Step Four

Close Again

Return to a time choice. This is what most reps forget.

“Would 2 or 4 work better?”
Discipline

Stay in your lane.

Your job is not to answer every solar question. Your job is to protect the appointment. The moment you start fully explaining equipment, utility rates, tax credits, warranties, batteries, net metering, financing, or proposal numbers — you are now doing the closer’s job at the wrong time with the wrong tools.

The customer starts analyzing before they have the full picture. Analysis creates more questions. More questions create doubt. Doubt kills momentum.

“

Answer briefly. Reframe the concern. Loop back to the appointment. Do not become the proposal at the home.

The Module

The 10-minute looping module.

Use this as a clean ten-minute section inside your training meeting. Teach it in this order.

  • 0–2 min
    Define looping
    Looping means handling the objection and returning to the close.
  • 2–4 min
    Why reps lose
    They answer the concern but forget to re-close.
  • 4–7 min
    Teach the five-part loop
    Acknowledge · Validate · Reframe · Rebuild value · Close again.
  • 7–9 min
    Demonstrate
    Run “I need to think about it” and “I am not interested.”
  • 9–10 min
    Set the standard
    Every objection response ends with a time choice or a controlled No close.
The Loop

The five-part loop.

1 · Acknowledge“I get that.”
2 · Validate“Most people feel that way at first.”
3 · Reframe“You are not deciding on solar today.”
4 · Rebuild value“The only question is whether the numbers are lower than your current bill.”
5 · Close again“Are you better around 2 or 4?”
“

If you answer the objection but do not close again, you did not handle the objection. You just had a conversation.

Limits

The two-loop limit.

Looping gives you control, but it has a limit. The goal is not to chase someone forever — it’s to professionally handle resistance and create a fair chance for the appointment.

02
Loops Maximum
After two strong loops, you book it or exit professionally.
01
No Close
Used after resistance, only if the assumptive didn’t land.
00
Desperate Lines
Past two loops, you start sounding needy. Exit cleanly.
Clean Exit Script

“Totally understand. Not every home is the right fit. I appreciate your time.”

Then leave calmly. Do not act irritated. Do not toss in one more desperate line from the sidewalk. Move to the next home.

The Playbook

Common objections.
The correct loop.

Tap any objection to expand the full response.

01
“I need to talk to my spouse.”
+

Teaching pointDon’t fight the spouse objection. Use it as the reason for the appointment.

If spouse is home: “Totally understand. Is he home right now? I’d rather give both of you the quick version so nobody has to repeat it wrong later. It’ll take two minutes.”

If spouse is not home: “Completely fair. That’s actually why we want both of you there for the quick sit-down. Nobody is deciding anything without them. The whole point is just to see if the home qualifies. Are evenings usually better when both of you are home?”

Then close“Got it. I have 6 or 7 tomorrow. Which one is better?”

02
“I am too busy.”
+

Teaching pointBusy is not a no. Busy means schedule.

“Totally get it. I am not trying to hold you up right now. That is actually why we schedule it. My guy comes back when it is convenient, shows you the numbers, and you can see if it is even worth your time. Are you usually better earlier or later in the day?”

If they say “come back another time”“Perfect. That’s what I’m trying to lock in so we don’t catch you at a bad time again. Tomorrow, would 2 or 4 be better?”

03
“We are not interested.”
+

Teaching pointReframe from “buy solar” to “qualify and compare numbers.”

“Totally fair. Most people say that because they think I am asking them to buy solar today. I am not. My job is just to see if the home would even qualify for the newer program. If the numbers are not lower than what you already pay, it would not make sense anyway. Would it be completely unreasonable just to see if your home qualifies?”

Close“My guy is already in the area tomorrow. Are you more of an afternoon or evening type?”

04
“I already looked into solar — it didn’t make sense.”
+

Teaching pointAgree first. Then separate the old program from the current conversation.

“Honestly, that makes sense. A lot of the older programs did not pencil out for people. That is exactly why we are checking homes again. The only reason this is worth a conversation is because the structure is different now. My guy can show you side by side if it is better or if it is still not worth it.”

Close“Would 2 or 4 be better tomorrow?”

05
“Can you leave me something?”
+

Teaching pointLiterature does not create certainty. Specific numbers do.

“Sure, I can leave you something. The problem is the paper will not tell you if your specific home qualifies or what your numbers look like. That is the part my guy has to run with you. It is quick. If it does not make sense, you say no. Are you usually home more in the afternoon or evening?”

Stronger version: “I can, but honestly it will not answer the only question that matters — whether your bill actually goes down. That is why we do the quick sit-down.”

06
“What is the catch?”
+

Teaching point“What is the catch?” usually means they are listening. Don’t panic.

“Good question. The catch is not every home qualifies, and if the numbers are not better than what you already pay, it would not make sense. That is exactly what the appointment is for. My guy shows you what it is, how it works, and whether it actually saves you money. If it does not, you do nothing.”

Close“He is here tomorrow. Are you better around 2 or 4?”

07
“How much does it cost?”
+

Teaching pointNever guess numbers. A guessed number creates a future cancellation.

“That is exactly what my guy shows you. It depends on the home, the usage, and whether the property qualifies. The important part is we are looking to replace what you already pay with something lower. If it is not lower, you would not do it anyway. That is why we run the numbers.”

Close“Are you guys usually better afternoons or evenings?”

08
“I do not want a loan.”
+

Teaching pointDo not over-explain financing. Protect the appointment.

“Totally understand. Most people do not want another loan either. That is why this is worth looking at. My guy can show you the structure and whether it is actually replacing the utility bill instead of adding a big new payment. If it does not make sense, you do not move forward.”

Close“He is in the area tomorrow. Would 2 or 4 work better?”

09
“We are moving soon.”
+

Teaching pointClarify before you disqualify.

“That makes sense. Depending on how soon, it may or may not be worth it. That is exactly why it is better to have my guy look at it quickly instead of guessing. Are you moving in the next few months or just sometime down the road?”

If down the road“Got it. Then it’s probably still worth seeing the numbers. Would tomorrow afternoon or evening be better?”

10
“We’re renting.” — polite exit
+

Renters do not qualify. Politely disengage — do not waste time pitching.

Exit“Got it — this program is only available for homeowners. I appreciate your time!”
